Trade Programs

Five Paths Into the Skilled Trades

Every SCI Trade program is built around what employers are actually hiring for — the hands-on skills, safety certifications, and real-world shop experience that get you working faster than any four-year degree ever could.

HVAC

Learn heating, ventilation, and air conditioning from the ground up — EPA guidelines, refrigerant handling, ductwork, air measurement, basic electricity, and system maintenance. SCI is an NCCER authorized testing site, so certification prep is baked in, not bolted on.

EPA 608 Cert Prep NCCER Testing Site Lab + Field Training
Welding

Train in SMAW (stick), GMAW (MIG), GTAW (TIG), and Flux-Core welding processes. Cover blueprint reading, metallurgy, AWS welding codes, and shop safety. SCI welding grads have landed jobs at companies like SpaceX. Real programs, real outcomes.

AWS Standards 4 Welding Processes Shop Lab Training
Electrical Technician

Train to install, repair, and maintain electrical systems across residential, commercial, and industrial settings. Covers OSHA safety standards, blueprint and schematic reading, wiring methods, and NEC code fundamentals. Instructors include veterans of the trade with 15+ years of field experience.

NCCER Cert Prep Residential + Industrial OSHA Safety Included
CDL-A Commercial Motor Vehicle Operator

Get behind the wheel with SCI's hands-on CDL-A program. Covers pre-trip inspections, in-cab procedures, backing maneuvers, FMCSA regulations, and road training — everything to pass your CDL exams at the Texas DMV. Graduates are driving commercially across the country.

CDL Exam Prep Road + Yard Training FMCSA Regulations
Automotive Service Technician

Train as an apprentice automotive technician — engines, brakes, transmissions, electrical systems, and diagnostics. SCI's apprenticeship model mirrors actual shop conditions from day one, so you're not starting from zero when you walk into your first job.

Apprenticeship Model Diagnostics + Repair Shop Bay Training

Why Southern Careers Institute

Built for People Who Work With Their Hands

SCI's trade programs are built around one idea: employers want people who can do the job on day one. Here's what makes SCI different from every other option in Texas.

Shop & Field Training from Day One

You're not sitting at a desk watching videos. SCI trade students train in actual shop and lab environments — handling tools, running equipment, and developing the physical skills that employers can't teach on the job site.

Get Working Faster

Trade programs at SCI are designed to move quickly — no general education requirements, no filler courses, no waiting around. You train for the job, not for a transcript. Most programs can be completed in under a year.

NCCER Certification Prep On Campus

SCI is an NCCER authorized testing site for trade programs — which means certification prep is built into the curriculum, not an afterthought. You prep and test in the same place you trained, with instructors who know the material cold.

Courses Built From an Employer's View

SCI designs every program with the question employers actually ask: can this person do the job? The curriculum is built to produce graduates who are more marketable than candidates from programs that prioritize theory over application.

OSHA Safety Standards Baked In

Every SCI trade program includes OSHA guidelines, personal protective equipment, and jobsite safety training. You won't just know how to do the work — you'll know how to do it without getting hurt or getting someone else hurt.

Instructors With Real Field Experience

SCI trade instructors aren't career academics — they've worked the trades. Whether it's an electrical tech with 15 years on the job or a CDL instructor who knows exactly what to do in a pre-trip, the knowledge in the room is real.

Accreditation & Recognition

Your Credential Has Weight

SCI is fully accredited and approved by state and national bodies — which matters when employers are looking at your resume and when you're sitting for certification exams.

Council on Occupational Education (COE)

Institutional accreditation recognized by the U.S. Secretary of Education — the same standard employer hiring teams look for.

Texas Workforce Commission Approved

Approved and regulated by the TWC Career Schools and Colleges division — ensuring programs meet Texas workforce standards.

NCCER Authorized Testing Site

SCI campuses are authorized by the National Center for Construction Education and Research (NCCER) to administer certification exams on-site for HVAC, Electrical, and Welding programs.

Texas Veterans Commission Approved

SCI is approved to train eligible veterans. GI Bill® benefits accepted. Military grants available for active duty, veterans, and eligible dependents.
