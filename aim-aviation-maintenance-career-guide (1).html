What Is FAA A&P Certification — and Why Does It Matter?

The FAA Airframe & Powerplant (A&P) certificate is the baseline credential for anyone who wants to legally inspect, repair, and maintain commercial or private aircraft in the United States. Without it, you can only do supervised work. With it, you can work independently at any licensed repair station, airline, MRO facility, or private aviation operator in the country.

The FAA requires a minimum of 1,900 hours of hands-on training to sit for the A&P exams — which is exactly why school selection matters. Not all programs structure those hours the same way, and the quality of your practical training directly impacts your exam readiness and job performance on day one.

The certificate covers two separate areas:

  • Airframe — the body, structure, flight controls, landing gear, and hydraulic systems
  • Powerplant — engines, fuel systems, electrical systems, and propellers

Most employers want both. AIM's program covers both.

Geography matters significantly. States with major airline hubs — Texas, Florida, Georgia, California — tend to offer both more openings and higher compensation. Commercial airline mechanics typically earn more than general aviation mechanics, and entry-level A&P techs at regional carriers commonly start in the $55K–$65K range, moving up quickly with experience.

Is AIM Right for You?

AIM tends to be a strong fit if you:

  • Want a hands-on career that doesn't require a 4-year degree
  • Are comfortable working with complex mechanical and electrical systems
  • Want a federally recognized credential that's portable across the entire US aviation industry
  • Prefer an accelerated schedule over a multi-year university program
  • Are open to relocation — aviation maintenance jobs concentrate near major airports

This is physically demanding work. You'll spend time on your feet, in confined spaces, and working with heavy components. The FAA also requires good vision (correctable is fine) and the ability to pass a background check for commercial airport work.
